New Zealand

Tags: Travel, New Zealand

Earlier this month I spent a fortnight in New Zealand, and now I’m over the inevitable post-travel illness. The trip was split into two parts: a week in Auckland with a side trip to Rotorua; followed by a week in Queenstown and its surrounds. I wouldn’t recommend this itinerary. Not because it was bad trip, quite the opposite. It was not long enough in either location. Best would be to hire a campervan (we just had a hire car for a few days) and make a 3+ week road trip from Auckland to Queenstown. The country is very pretty and easy to to travel (for an Australian at least).

Auckland seems like a pleasant, liveable city. Less spread out than Perth (smaller population too, despite being the largest city in NZ) with more happening (judging by the posters). It even has a nice nearby island, Waiheke, with beaches and wineries. There was activity in the CBD and many decent multicultural food options. We had some very good West Chinese noodles. Some of this may be because there are a great number of tourists. We were told 7% of the NZ economy is tourism!

Tourism is very apparent on reaching Queenstown. The place seems built for sightseeing. And such sights! This is a beautiful part of the world, no surprise it is often used in films. I had heard that the NZ economy was not doing so well. However, Queenstown and surrounding towns seem to be thriving. Lots of building, lots of people, lots to do. Interestingly we met nearly no locals. The people serving tourists in hotels, restaurants and on tours were all recent arrivals. On our tour to Milford Sound, the guide told us he had been in the country only a year, and walked into that job right after arrival.

Old Films

Tags: General, Memories

Some thoughts…

I recently watched Last Year at Marienbad (part of the 60’s French New Wave) on Kanopy (a great streaming service if you have a library card). I enjoyed the film despite it being slow and without much of a plot (a man tries to convince a women that they know each other for 90 minutes). There was some clever film making and the more subtle cuts worked well to disorient the viewer (or at least me). This movie is considered a classic. I’ve heard about it many times over the decades. Would it still be if released for the first time today? I doubt it, despite thinking it’s a good movie. It is too similar to many arthouse or experimental films I’ve seen.

The French New Wave were known for breaking accepted movie conventions. Now their rule-breaking is part of the conventions. Last Year at Marienbad reminded me a little of Before Sunrise. Parts from Spike Lee or Tarantino movies remind me of another French New Wave film, Breathless. I wish people wouldn’t copy the style of introspective dream sequences from 8½ (because I think they rarely succeed). Its not only the more arty filmmakers. The excellent over-the-top action of Hardboiled spawned a wave of inferior over-the-top 90’s action movies. More successfully, the gritty survival-action of The Raid inspired great action in modern movies (like John Wick).

Years ago I saw Citizen Kane because it was acclaimed as a classic. I wasn’t impressed. It didn’t make much of an impact. I guessed the ending and it didn’t seem that special. Later I learnt how films were different prior to Citizen Kane and what did actually made it special and groundbreaking. Films now are made by people influenced by people influenced by people influenced by Citizen Kane. The lessons have infused into the background knowledge of people both making and watching films, without them ever having necessarily seen the origin.

The best example of this is Psycho. Not the 1960 original that broke new ground and shocked (and yes, I’ve seen Peeping Tom, but they were made concurrently on different continents, so how much could they effect each other?). Instead the 1998 Gus Van Sant remake. The two are almost exactly the same shot-for-shot and word-for-word. So if the first is a classic, the same movie nearly 40 years later must also be good? Nope, the reviews for the remake are awful, it bombed at the box office, and is now largely forgotten. What is the difference? It can’t be entirely the audience’s changing tastes. Critics often praised the original while condemning the remake. Is it the original was first and was original; the remake just a well made copy?

In gamedev there is a saying that ideas are worthless, execution is everything (I’ve heard similar among tech startups). Watch Last Year at Marienbad reminded me that sometimes an original idea is worth something too.

AI Coding Week 3

Tags: Technical, Claude

Week 3 of Claude Code test is complete (week 1 here and week 2 here). It is unlikely I’ll write a week 4 report. Not because I am unhappy, but the opposite. I think this experiment has shown that there is some value in the current coding assistants for me. Thus the next task is to think about how I can use them productively (given their advantages and flaws), which is probably more than a one week job.

Speaking of flaws the week started with a stark reminder. I tried out Claude Code on the Web, it did not go well. The idea is to provide an isolated environment accessible anywhere so that you can set Claude loose without concern. However, I quickly found a concern! While setting up my current Scala project I discovered the environment did not have the sbt build tool installed. This is completely normal, and I have installed it many times, it is a simple process. I asked Claude to install it (as I couldn’t find a way to get terminal access for myself) and walked away to do something else. After 10 or so minutes it had burned through 9% of my weekly quota failing to install sbt!

When Claude has a problem it keeps trying things. It doesn’t stop and reassess the instruction. In this case it is likely it was just not possible; that the environment was too locked down. Claude didn’t care. I was unimpressed because this is Claude’s own environment, but apparently it doesn’t know its own limitations in its own environment. Also, it doesn’t seem to be aware of its own quota/usage (I asked if it could be, and it replied no). So just just burns through it.

After calming down for a day, I got back to testing. Claude was tasked to create a Godot based prototype client for the little game previously created. Behold…

About as good as I could do, but much faster. If I wanted to sell copies of the game, it would need to be massively improved with a prettier interface. That would realistically require a great deal of time, testing and iteration if I did it, or (like with my last game) paying someone with better UI design sense to do it. Although. for just getting started with development and testing work, this is fine. So a success.

As that task barely cost a third of my weekly quota, I then assigned it to create a website to import the datafiles of various fitness apps that I use and combine the results into a single view. It did it so well I suspect this is similar to Claude’s standard training jobs. Then with the little remaining quota I questioned Claude about the best way to structure projects to keep its usage low. Its responses matched my anecdotal experiences over the last few weeks.

So the experiment is over. Not sure what I will do with Claude next week, perhaps some little long abandoned toy side-projects. If I decide to pay for it in future, I will need to have a plan with long term goals. It can be very easy to just treat this as a fun game, but if that’s all I use it for, real games would be better (and cheaper). If nothing else it has forced me to think about how I develop software (in order to effectively instruct Claude) and what I should be doing with my time.

Itch Games

Tags: Games, Video Games

Given my annoyance at Itch.io (they still have not paid me!) I thought it time to work through my backlog of games there, so I can abandon my account. No links to Itch (is PageRank still active?).

Astrologaster (4 hrs) - A well-made story game of an astrologer trying to become a doctor in 16th century London. Based partially on real people/events! The story seems linear, and a little too long. By the end I was just choosing diagnoses at random. It didn’t seem to make much difference, the story continued the way it wanted. I liked the humour and historicity (I looked a few people/events up on Wikipedia). Mildly interesting game elevated by surprisingly good production values.

A Mortician’s Tale (1 hr) - Very short (I finished in an hour) story game about what happens to our bodies after death. Partly epistolary, part point and click, the story is very linear, but that doesn’t matter as it’s finished quickly (the secret to good story games in my opinion). A good game that meets its ambitions.

Flash Point (1 hr) - Computer adaptation of the cooperative boardgame of putting out fires and rescuing people. It felt clumsy, repetitive and random. I just couldn’t get into it.

From Orbit (2hrs) - A roguelike RTS, that sees your spaceship trying to escape from “something” (not clear what) by travelling through a series of planets. On each planet you have to battle waves of enemies while collecting resources. Over time you can power up your crew and gain new crew members. At least I think that is the game - there is no tutorial or instructions so I just had to work it out as I went. A bit clunky and repetitive, this feels more like a decent beginning of the game development process rather than a finished product.

Attrition: Tactical Fronts (2hrs) - I feel for the developers of this fast turn-based tactics game set in WW1. It reminds me of my game in that there is something here, but the game just doesn’t quite get there. It seems to be online multiplayer focussed, but since it is 7 years old, no one is around to play. I went through just over 2/3rds of the solo campaign and faced no significant challenge, winning all easily - largely due to the AI making regular game ending mistakes. Entertaining for a short time.

Death and Taxes (4hrs) - I loved the idea of this point-and-click story game where you play as a Grim Reaper (apparently there are many of them) choosing who should die in a bureaucratic office run by Fate. It also works well in practice too. At first I wasn’t sure what I was supposed to be doing, but soon discovered that is part of the game. I played through once, enjoyed it, and don’t plan to do so again despite some replayability built in. Very good.

The Stillness of the Wind (1hr) - A farming simulator where an old woman tends a farm alone, while receiving increasingly worrisome letters from her departed family. Not really my sort of game, and I found the controls clunky.

AI Coding Week 2

Tags: Technical, Claude

Week Two of testing out Claude code is complete (see week 1 here). Actually, the week ended on Wednesday when my weekly quota ended. That is despite not using it on Saturday at all, and only a little on Sunday. The 7 quota seems to be more like a 3 day quota. I wonder if that is a sales technique?

This (half) week I tried out creating a greenfield project. A simple non-graphical web game that has little commercial prospects, so I don’t care how it goes. Claude just finished a prototype in its allotted time, shaking out a few issues - a worthwhile result.

First I asked it to help out with designing the system. I gave it a couple of paragraphs describing the game and some technical constraints. It then asked long series of very good questions before outputting some design docs and an implementation plan. It mostly understood the brief, and the plan was decent. I was very impressed with this stage, I would happily use Claude again help the design and planning phase of development. The main benefit is that I had Claude log all its decisions and work. Now I have a complete list of ideas, alternatives and the reasons decisions were made - something I’m usually too lazy to bother with (and then regret later).

Then I had Claude work step-by-step (24 steps in the plan) in development. It ran out of quota on the last step (online deployment), but I can run it locally so its not too bad. I looked a little at the code produced, but apart from code duplication and superfluous config, it looked ok. So I mostly let it do its own thing. Often it would find its own mistakes, eventually.

The end result is… decent. Very simple gameplay and a frontend that is “backend developer” quality (so the same as me). It is largely what I asked it to do. I haven’t found any bugs, but do have a long list of improvements and ideas. Thus, I would declare it a successful prototype. Claude produced more than I could have done in 3 days for sure, and probably more than I could do in a solid week’s work. Although there are a few things I would have improved along the way, so I like to think my quality would be higher.

There are two big issues I see:

  • Quota limits: At first I thought this week it was getting more done considering the quota used. However, it just seems to be small projects (such as the initial greenfield work) result in a smaller context, and thus lower quota usage. I’m not sure what the solution is. Maybe break projects into smaller components. Maybe avoid long running sessions (don’t do more than a couple of compactions). Some experimentation is required.
  • It also makes me want to let it free reign - or as Claude calls it, YOLO mode! Although I’m way to scared and careful with my data to allow it. I’ll try looking at isolated environments like Claude Code Web or Sprites.io to see if they work well enough.

Next week I’ll try getting Claude to create something in Godot (maybe a client for this week’s game). If it can do that then I can see this being useful, despite the limitations.
