Dwellingup

Tags: Travel, Australia

Over my birthday I made a weekend trip down to Dwellingup at fairly short notice. The days were spend hiking in the local Jarrah forest, and the nights spent collapsing after walking for hours. There are not enough photos for a slideshow, so I have just put a few below along with some notes. It was a pleasant weekend, and a good reminder that such places can be close to the city. Perhaps I should head out to them more often.

  • The weather was cooler than usual and perfect for hiking: warm but not hot. Long may this continue (although the forecast says the traditional blazing WA summer is about to hit soon). This meant we could appreciate the beauty of the forest rather than feel like we were on a forced march before inevitably melting in the sun.
  • For the first time in a while, the flies were annoying (no mozzies though).
  • There appears to be a single restaurant in Dwellingup open for dinner, and by restaurant I mean pub, and by dinner I mean the kitchen shuts at 7:15pm. It was standard WA country pub menu at the standard quality, ok but nothing special. For the second night we drove down to Pinjarrah (just a short 20 min away) and had a surprisingly good curry.
  • Most of the hiking paths were easy to follow (many were shared with the Munda Biddi Trail), but for while there was just a vaguely cleared track through the bush. Inevitably we got lost and added a few kilometers before getting back on course. We also saw many fallen trail markers laying in the ground (maybe one we didn’t see was the cause of the detour). I should probably invest in some non-phone network navigation device, because this keeps happening.

Bunbury

Tags: Travel, Australia

A couple of weeks after travelling to Sri Lanka, I went a few hours south to spend a long weekend in Bunbury and its surrounds. It is amazing how it becomes noticeably cooler and greener with such a short journey. Walking around Bunbury is easy and pretty, although you are finished quickly since the town is surprisingly small. There is one main street, a couple of malls and a few streets full of big box stores. Restaurant selection is limited. The farmers market is still a great food-only supermarket. Prices seemed largely the same as Perth. Overall it felt like a large successful country town, which I suppose it is.

Other places visited:

  • The Donnybrook Apple Festival is very popular, it felt like there were thousands of visitors taking over this small town! And it does not seem to have much to do with apples - there was just a single stall selling apples (very tasty!) and few others selling “related” products. The event seems like a cross between a small agricultural show and and a large standard market.
  • Australind felt like a commuter suburb. There was a nice Italian restaurant though (forgotten its name).
  • Busselton has become a tourist town. Despite being much smaller, the main shopping street was nearly as large as Bunbury’s. However, the shops there felt like they were mostly for visitors.

Sri Lanka

Tags: Travel, Sri Lanka

At the star of April I spent a fortnight in Sri Lanka for a wedding. Perhaps half the time was spent on travelling (see below) and the wedding activities, but we managed some sightseeing along the way. Overall it seems like a nice place to visit, although I’m in no rush to go back (despite missing some good stuff like Sigiriya). People have said it is similar to the Bali of 30 years ago. I can see that, it seems to be a good place to relax by the beach. Some thoughts:

  • There are surprisingly few restaurants everywhere we went (except the Negombo tourist strip). We probably only had 3 meals at local eateries, and that took some effort. I thought perhaps they were there, but I wasn’t looking for the right signs. However, I checked with various online maps, then confirmed by talking to locals and other tourists. Apparently Sri Lankans don’t tend to eat out often and tourists spend most of their time at touristy places (so outside those places good luck!).
  • Colombo is jarring mix of run down building and new towers, often very close to eachother.
  • You are dangerously gem deficient! Walking around the capital we were regularly approached by initially friendly men who quickly told us that we were lucky to be in town that day as there was a special one-day only sale at the best gem store in town. We must go! This was over many different days and stores. One was a little aggressive, but all eventually accepted we weren’t going anywhere with them.
  • Expect to pay more as a non-local, sometimes by a lot. For example, when a taxi wanted more from us than a equivalent Australian taxi (which are not cheap) we tried Uber and got a car at tenth of the asking price.
  • Security sometimes can be very tight, other times non-existent. Going into hotels and shopping malls we went through metal detectors and bag checks. However, other places like train stations had no security, often not even a ticket check. There was no visible security on the streets either (although it still felt safe). Presumably this is a holdover from the long finished civil war and more recent protests.
  • The train network is extensive and amazingly cheap, but incredibly slow and often delayed. Don’t be overly concerned about going in the lower classes as long as you have a set seat. We went in reserved 3rd class for one trip - it was fine and was not that much worse than 1st (no A/C, but we had the windows open). Overall we spent two nearly entire days travelling a few hundred kilometers and back again.
  • The surrounds of Hatton are pretty (not so much the town itself), but there is not much else. The main site seems to be Adam’s Peak a sacred site at the top of a mountain and the destination for a lot of internal tourism. It is extremely busy!
  • Negombo has a strip along the beach full of bars, restaurants, hotels, and shops of local arts. It seemed pleasant but definitely tourist focused. We had a very good fish meal there in a restaurant just off the strip on our last night.
  • It was hard to find good flights to Sri Lanka. Avoiding budget airlines and overly long flights meant we had two options. Both arrived and departed near midnight. Relatively cheap though.

Focus

Tags: Memories

Recently I’ve thinking about my previous work and how it affected my state of mind. I think I can see a pattern. Perhaps I’m just telling stories to explain myself. Is it true, maybe, or perhaps in 10 years I’ll think differently.

All but one or two (depending on how generous I feel) places I have worked have “betrayed” me. Three if you count the years I worked for myself on Concealed Intent. Betrayed in the sense that certain promises (implicit or explicit) about how things would work were made at the start, but eventually they changed the rules. Sometimes this was because the person making the promise didn’t have the power to keep it; sometimes because they lied; sometimes because the situation changed so the so my work conditions necessarily did too. Occasionally the change came quickly, Dresdner told me they made false representations (unknowingly, they claimed) to me during the interview process when I turned up on the first day at work! Others took longer. Only once was I asked if I was ok with the change of conditions - I said no and we arranged redundancy on good terms. Well done MCPS-PRS, that is the correct way to handle it.

Looking back over that time, I see a direct link between my work quality and my feelings towards the workplace. When I start I am full of focus. Hardworking, loyal’ish (as far as it goes in the modern workplace). Willing to do reasonably long hours - not junior Investment Banker hours, but I have done my fair share of 60+ hour weeks, extra weekends, late nights and early mornings. When the betrayal comes that all changes. I become disillusioned and demotivated. This lasts until I leave, never has an employer recovered the situation. At Dresdner, I should have quit on that first day. Instead, there was nearly 18 months of bitterness, annoyance and poor performance. I have done some excellent work, but it has all been relatively early in my time at a company.

So far, so unsurprising.

Perhaps less common, I believe there is also a strong correlation between what I am like to be around and what state I am in work wise. When focussed, the work is all that matters. I can become irritable at anything or anyone stopping me from getting the job done. Sometimes that is my work colleagues or environment, but more usually it is things and people outside work. For instance, I am far less likely to go to social events or be resentful towards the ones I do attend. This is because I am glad to be doing that work and enjoying the process - I do it willingly and happily. So when the betrayal comes I feel like I have been scammed, but then I do it all again at the next place.

When disillusioned, I am more relaxed and pleasant to be around. It is during these times I go looking for social connections and meet new people. It’s all relative though, I still probably do those things less than most people.

This has also been the case with more personal projects. It is not about someone paying me for the work. I have felt the same about Concealed Intent, my abandoned PhD and even just some things I did at home for “fun”. They also became the centre of my life for a while. I think of them like work. During the years I was making Concealed Intent, I considered myself to be working (while some other people didn’t) and remember being annoyed if I couldn’t put the time I wanted into it. Even if the “distraction” was going on holiday with my partner. There is a strong correlation in my life to meeting someone and being involved with them during the time of work disillusionment, matched by a similar correspondence between times of work focus and breaking up with those same people.

Maybe this is why I feel generally happier and nicer to be around now. I don’t consider myself to be working since leaving Substantive 5 years ago.
