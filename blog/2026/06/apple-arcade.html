Apple Arcade Games (Part 1)

Tags: Games, Video Games

I bought myself a new iPhone at the start of the year and it came with a bunch of free trials for Apple services including for Apple Arcade, their game subscription service. I took a look at what is available. The vast majority are casual games and not my thing, but there were more than a few games that I had on my Steam wishlist. I signed up quickly and had 3 months to get through them. Here are the reviews.

Balatro (7 hrs) - Well made poker-based deckbuilder roguelite. Collect cards to use in hands scored as in poker to earn enough points to beat the blind. That’s largely it, which is both the brilliance and the problem. The supposed main game activity (playing hands) is extremely simple. So the game becomes about collecting jokers which change the rules of scoring. So its really all about playing the metagame, and that can be addictive as there are a huge number of options and they are unlocked slowly. I found myself quitting a run if I didn’t get the right jokers at an early stage. I enjoyed my time playing this game and may play some more on occasion, but I’m opting out of the progression grind.

Mini Motorways (2 hrs) - A sequel to Mini Metro (which I have played, but can’t find a review on here). Add roads to an abstract map of various cities to allow travel from residences to workplaces. I played quite a bit of Mini Metro, and this feels exactly the same, only with a worse theme (I don’t like cars). Still, it is well-made and if you haven’t played one before they are worth a try, but this one is not for me.

Dicey Dungeons (4 hrs) - Defeat the nefarious Lady Luck in a dungeon delving reality show by collecting actions activated by dice rolling in this fast playing deckbuilder-like rogue-like game. Cute game, well made, but it feels quite simple. Once the characters are unlocked (after an hour or so) there didn’t seem to be much more depth. A great start that fades out.

Unpacking (4 hrs) - Clever story game where the entire gameplay is unpacking moving boxes and putting stuff away in your new residence. Tells a simple life story quickly and effectively, just through what is the background in other games. You poke around the places being moved into and begin to see patterns in what is being unpacked, or what is missing. It also made me wonder if I’m normal - the game disallowed placing a particular item in the same place it is in at my house! It is short at four hours for me to complete it. Good game, worth a try.

Turmoil (4 hrs) - A realtime casual strategy game of drilling for oil and then pumping and selling it in the 1800s. You are alone on your map, but after each level (around 5 minutes) your score is compared to the other players/AIs. The levels combine towards a campaign with the rules/scenario changing slightly. This is a well-made almost-casual short game. I enjoyed it, but there is not enough depth (pun intended) to keep me playing.

Dredge (6 hrs) - Do you like fetch quests? Well Dredge is all fetch quests as far as I can tell. It is a cute fishing game in an eerie setting. But not too eerie. It feels like the game is trying to give a sense of dread and foreboding, but never really committing. I played the same game for six hours without any fear or negative consequence (including some night fishing). The worst outcome I had was a little damage to my boat - quickly and cheaply fixed. This is a well produced game, but the repetitive gameplay and unthreatening story was not enough for me. Baby’s first Lovecraftian horror game.

Cult of the Lamb (7 hrs) - Form a cult of cute creatures to worship “The One Who Waits” and fight the followers of the old faith, sacrificing the occasional member of you own cult along the way. This darkly humorous game is a mash of of two genres: a small scale city-builder as you slowly establish your cult; and a realtime rogue-like battler as you sally forth against the heretics. This is an excellent well-made game, but I have two big gripes. Firstly I find the cult compound section quickly becomes a grind (perhaps this is dealt with buildings available further down the tech-tree?). Secondly and more importantly, the controls for the rogue-like battles are atrocious on the iPad. I found myself constantly annoyed at the game for doing the wrong this because my fingers were not in exactly the right place on the screen. It caused me to stop playing an otherwise enjoyable game. Good game, but do NOT play on an iPad!

Civilization 7 (5 hrs) - I have played most of the Civilization games about leading a building a civilization through time - it is the original 4X game. I found them all addictive with that “just one more turn” compulsion… until this one. I not sure why. It seems very well made, everything I’m used to is here. I played a couple of starts (usually the best bit before the busywork starts) and just lost interest. Maybe I have just played too many of these now.

Monster Train (17 hrs) - I have definitely not played too many deckbuilder roguelikes, because here is another and I loved it. I could easily imagine sinking many more hours into this one. The theme is silly - you are leading a train of demonic creatures through the circles of hell to restart the fires (hell has frozen over, duh). Although that doesn’t matter because basically you choosing cards for creatures and spells to play against your angelic enemies - think gameplay like Slay The Spire. Most of the gameplay is fairly standard, with a few new features like having three levels to fight on with you choosing on which level to place your creatures, the enemy starts at the bottom and moves up each turn until they attack the train engine itself. Good game, lots of replayability.

Outlanders 2 (4 hrs) - A pleasant little rural “settlement builder”. The game is mission-based and the missions are short (I got through 5) so it is probably great for a casual builder fix. Well-made, nice, but uninspiring.

So after all those, when the the trial ends in a week will I pay for Apple Arcade? No. I played all the games I wanted at the start, plus a few extras. Only two of those I would consider continuing to play (Balatro & Monster Train), but I’m also fine stopping.
