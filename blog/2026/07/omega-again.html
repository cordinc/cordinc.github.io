Omega Again

Tags: Games, Video Games

A little while ago a member of the Grogpod podcast about roguelike games contacted me out of the blue on Reddit, asking if I’d like to share any memorable experiences with Omega to be read-out on an episode. Omega is an 80’s old style roguelike which I first played at uni in the 90’s (and occasionally since). Ten years ago I wrote a review and made some videos about it. The podcast planned an episode on the original wave of roguelikes; choosing the relatively obscure Omega as one of the three they would play. Below is what I sent them, and the episode it features in is Episode 100 Part 2 - Moria, Hack, Larn, Omega.

It is 1993, I am playing Omega.
It is 2004, I am playing Omega.
It is 2016, I am playing Omega.
It is 2026, once again I am playing Omega. 
Why? I played most of the old roguelikes while avoiding study in the 90's, but Omega is the only one I regularly return to. It's not the best of its genre: the inventory system is, if I'm being polite, unusual; the early game RNG, brutal; the late game too soft; and I've hit more than a few bugs along my journeys. However, it was Omega's scale that attracted me to the game. There is a sense that a whole world (or at least an island) exists around the dungeons that provide most of the usual action. There is a town to investigate which has more happening than just being a place to restock. Outside the town is countryside to explore with villages, wild animals, and of course the occasional dungeon too. Omega is an early attempt at an open world.

These early rogue-likes are a hive of experimentation. They are the indie games of their time. So many ideas were thrown together by people trying to recreate their tabletop RPG sessions. For me, Omega succeeded more in this than it failed. It feels like there is a world continuing along regardless of the player actions. This isn’t true of course, but it is the impression that counts, and that is enough for me.
