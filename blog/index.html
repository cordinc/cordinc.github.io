Itch Anniversary

Tags: General

Yesterday was exactly one year since I requested a payout of the money accrued at Itch.io. Of course this did not go as planned, and no payment was forthcoming. I had it down on my calendar to review the situation, and when the reminder popped up, my heart sank. You can make you own assessment of what motivated Itch to act.

AI Coding Week X

Tags: Technical, Claude

Not sure how long I’ve been trying out AI-assisted coding. There was the first 4 weeks (weeks 1, 2, 3), followed by a long gap, and then I started again. Probably its has been a total of 10 weeks by now, but I’m not sure. Let’s just call it week X. It seems like a good time to reflect again, as another project has just finished and its worth noting what I have learned. Short version is that I’m still impressed, but a little less so than before as some of its fiddly realities become clearer.

When I renewed my subscription to Claude Pro, I decided to get things going by finishing an old unfinished project. A few years ago I started a Scala version of the Ink interpreter (a language to write stories in games). After implementing about half of the tutorial, it started getting much harder and I abandoned the side-project. Based on my first go at AI coding, I thought it could get it finished in maybe two weeks, then I would move on. It took over 6 weeks.

At first I thought it was me. Had the month I had been doing other things made me forget and overestimate Claude’s ability? My weekly usage was evaporating so quickly. Once I asked it to debug an issue and it went around in circles burning through my entire 5-hour usage allowance in 20 minutes without a fix. I was so angry I almost ragequit and considered asking for a refund. But I didn’t. Instead I went online and found many people with the same problems. It definitely seems like Anthropic had changed something and made it much less useful and/or more expensive. Indeed a week later the company announced that there were bugs in the system, that they had now fixed. The system has gotten somewhat better since then; returning to usefulness. I would say it feels about as “intelligent” as those first promising weeks, but it definitely seems like my usage allowance is less. Now, it can do the tasks I give it, but my 5-hour allowance is always gone in well under an hour of usage. It also seems highly variable, some days lots get done, others very little.

Reading other people’s similar experiences, two things become clear. Firstly, Anthropic is constantly tinkering with their system. These changes do not seem particularly well directed or communicated. I feel like I’m blind testing the system for them and paying for the privilege (not a great customer experience). Secondly, all of AI-assisted coding acts like a cargo cult. There is lots of advice on how to make the most of these AIs and its often contradictory or not relevant or just doesn’t work or outdated (or soon to be outdated by another tweak!). All advice seems to be anecdotal. Much of it seems to be based on asking the AIs what they think (hmmm).

Despite the above, here are some of my anecdotal observations on AI coding assistants:

  • They are fast and on a good day can produce in under a hour something that would take me a multiple of that.
  • On small (10 min or so) constrained tasks they perform very well. If I give them a broken test that exercises a bug and ask them to fix it, then 90% of the time it is done quickly and efficiently.
  • Mechanical refactors are a waste of usage. They will do them, but slower than me and cost a large amount of usage in the process.
  • NEVER ask them to do more than 1 thing at a time (eg. fix these three bugs) they will get lost.
  • The larger the task, the more corners they will cut and the more careful you need to be!
  • They need to be constantly reminded to create tests and to not change existing (correct) tests. Once in order to “fix” a bug, Claude just changed the test to something completely wrong. Now I have multiple layers of instructions to never do that.
  • They write like a junior dev being paid by the number of lines produced. So much code, much of it unnecessary.
  • They are very bad learners! An example, Claude creates a plan for a new feature, but it doesn’t seem right to me. I ask about it and Claude says there is a better way, “make errors uncompilable”, as in its user-specified (by me) instructions. If it knew that was in its instructions, why did it not use it the first time? So I get it to do it again and it’s better. On a similar task the very next day, it forgets and does the same thing again. I question it, and it goes, yes of course “make errors uncompilable”!
  • Telling them to remember does not work. They need to be constantly reminded. Now my prompts often include a bunch of useful tips or coding philosophies so they always have them readily to hand.
  • If I’m not careful, they can easily create more code than I can usefully review or understand. This has already happened a few times in this project.
  • They are excellent at creating/updating notes, task lists, designs and other documentation. In general, they are good organisers.

That last point is one of the main reasons I will continue using AIs. Recently in my work I was awful at remembering why something was done or what tasks remained. I think I was better when younger and working professionally on the same project every day. Now I’m older and some time can pass between coding sessions so things get lost (and/or my memory is worse). Knowing the task list and reasoning is always there actually helps motivate me to work more regularly. Definitely a huge benefit as just turning up and moving slightly forward every week makes it easier to keep going as a habit (something I lost in recent years). I’ve also come to a good accommodation with the usage allowances. I do 3-4 hours work a day with regular breaks (across the 5-hour usage window) for 4-5 days/week (stopping when the weekly usage is exhausted). So normally I have Monday/Tuesday off, useful for completing other tasks (like writing this post).

What’s next? Time to try a larger project, something hopefully more useful. It it works out, then it will be its own topic. Thus this will be the last of these AI Coding posts for the foreseeable future.

Westworld

Tags: General

I recently decided to finish watching Westworld. I watched the first couple of season when they were the released, and my opinions seemed to match public perception: the first season was excellent; the second was a great disappointment and a slide towards mediocrity. It has been some time since the last two seasons were released, but I understand that the general opinion is that they did not reverse the descent.

Well I liked season 3 (however, season 4 was not good). I think it opened up the setting in an interesting manner. The story was not as good as season 1, but we got a better understanding of how the wider world outside the park worked. It was an ambitious attempt to show something new within a familiar Westworld story, and it mostly delivered. It felt like a modern cyberpunk (as opposed to the 80’s nostalgia of most recent cyberpunk).

In an ahistorical story (like all fantasy and most sci-fi), the wider environment usually seems to exist solely for the purpose of moving the story forward. I do not normally get a sense that these are worlds that exist outside of that particular movie or TV show (books tend to be better). It is a notable achievement when there is a show that does feel like it has a complete world (that existed before the start and will continue after the end of the story being told). Game of Thrones and The Expanse are recent examples.

Westworld season 1 was not a show like this, the setting was very much built for the purposes of the story, literally! So respect to the writers for the difficult and thankless task (most people will not care, and that is fine) of retrofitting a fuller, more interesting world onto the show. I appreciate the inclination to try something difficult, even if it doesn’t completely succeed. For a similar reason I have a better opinion of the second Matrix movie than most people (sooo much exposition, but at least for a good cause).

New Zealand

Tags: Travel, New Zealand

Earlier this month I spent a fortnight in New Zealand, and now I’m over the inevitable post-travel illness. The trip was split into two parts: a week in Auckland with a side trip to Rotorua; followed by a week in Queenstown and its surrounds. I wouldn’t recommend this itinerary. Not because it was bad trip, quite the opposite. It was not long enough in either location. Best would be to hire a campervan (we just had a hire car for a few days) and make a 3+ week road trip from Auckland to Queenstown. The country is very pretty and easy to to travel (for an Australian at least).

Auckland seems like a pleasant, liveable city. Less spread out than Perth (smaller population too, despite being the largest city in NZ) with more happening (judging by the posters). It even has a nice nearby island, Waiheke, with beaches and wineries. There was activity in the CBD and many decent multicultural food options. We had some very good West Chinese noodles. Some of this may be because there are a great number of tourists. We were told 7% of the NZ economy is tourism!

Tourism is very apparent on reaching Queenstown. The place seems built for sightseeing. And such sights! This is a beautiful part of the world, no surprise it is often used in films. I had heard that the NZ economy was not doing so well. However, Queenstown and surrounding towns seem to be thriving. Lots of building, lots of people, lots to do. Interestingly we met nearly no locals. The people serving tourists in hotels, restaurants and on tours were all recent arrivals. On our tour to Milford Sound, the guide told us he had been in the country only a year, and walked into that job right after arrival.

Old Films

Tags: General, Memories

Some thoughts…

I recently watched Last Year at Marienbad (part of the 60’s French New Wave) on Kanopy (a great streaming service if you have a library card). I enjoyed the film despite it being slow and without much of a plot (a man tries to convince a women that they know each other for 90 minutes). There was some clever film making and the more subtle cuts worked well to disorient the viewer (or at least me). This movie is considered a classic. I’ve heard about it many times over the decades. Would it still be if released for the first time today? I doubt it, despite thinking it’s a good movie. It is too similar to many arthouse or experimental films I’ve seen.

The French New Wave were known for breaking accepted movie conventions. Now their rule-breaking is part of the conventions. Last Year at Marienbad reminded me a little of Before Sunrise. Parts from Spike Lee or Tarantino movies remind me of another French New Wave film, Breathless. I wish people wouldn’t copy the style of introspective dream sequences from 8½ (because I think they rarely succeed). Its not only the more arty filmmakers. The excellent over-the-top action of Hardboiled spawned a wave of inferior over-the-top 90’s action movies. More successfully, the gritty survival-action of The Raid inspired great action in modern movies (like John Wick).

Years ago I saw Citizen Kane because it was acclaimed as a classic. I wasn’t impressed. It didn’t make much of an impact. I guessed the ending and it didn’t seem that special. Later I learnt how films were different prior to Citizen Kane and what did actually made it special and groundbreaking. Films now are made by people influenced by people influenced by people influenced by Citizen Kane. The lessons have infused into the background knowledge of people both making and watching films, without them ever having necessarily seen the origin.

The best example of this is Psycho. Not the 1960 original that broke new ground and shocked (and yes, I’ve seen Peeping Tom, but they were made concurrently on different continents, so how much could they effect each other?). Instead the 1998 Gus Van Sant remake. The two are almost exactly the same shot-for-shot and word-for-word. So if the first is a classic, the same movie nearly 40 years later must also be good? Nope, the reviews for the remake are awful, it bombed at the box office, and is now largely forgotten. What is the difference? It can’t be entirely the audience’s changing tastes. Critics often praised the original while condemning the remake. Is it the original was first and was original; the remake just a well made copy?

In gamedev there is a saying that ideas are worthless, execution is everything (I’ve heard similar among tech startups). Watch Last Year at Marienbad reminded me that sometimes an original idea is worth something too.
